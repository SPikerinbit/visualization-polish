\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relatedwork}

\stitle{Declarative Semantic Query Processing.}
Integrating LLMs as first-class relational operators has enabled declarative analytics over unstructured text. LOTUS~\cite{DBLP:journals/pvldb/PatelJPGAGZ25} formalizes semantic filter, join, aggregation, and top-$K$ operators within a DataFrame interface, while SEMA~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2603-11622} and Unify~\cite{DBLP:journals/pvldb/WangLWXL25} embed semantic operator pipelines and intra-operator optimizations inside analytical query engines. A complementary line of work reduces invocation costs for semantic filters through calibrated proxy cascades and distilled models, including BARGAIN~\cite{DBLP:journals/pacmmod/ZeighamiSP25}, ScaleDoc~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2509-12610}, Task Cascades~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2601-05536}, and batched predicate evaluation~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2603-04799}. Whereas row-independent predicates evaluate tuples in isolation, semantic \textsf{ORDER BY} must establish global relative order across tuples under a bounded context window $m$, coupling cross-batch scheduling with state reuse.

\stitle{LLM Ranking Paradigms and Listwise Operators.}
Early zero-shot LLM rankers explored pointwise scoring~\cite{DBLP:conf/emnlp/DrozdovZD0RWAIM23,DBLP:conf/emnlp/SachanLJAYPZ22}, pairwise tournament or graph aggregation~\cite{DBLP:conf/acl/LuoCH024,DBLP:conf/naacl/QinJHZWYSLLMWB24}, setwise heap selection~\cite{DBLP:conf/sigir/ZhuangZKZ24}, and fixed-stride listwise reranking over small retrieved slates~\cite{DBLP:conf/emnlp/0001YMWRCYR23,DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2305-02156,DBLP:conf/ecir/HouZLLXMZ24}. Recent database studies adapt listwise prompting to full relations. ListK~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2603-17223} develops tournament selection and multi-pivot quickselect and quicksort over fine-tuned rankers, using analytical recall--latency models to select macro-pipelines; however, pivot partitioning expends window slots on coarse bucketing rather than preserving full local permutations, and its quickselect operator outputs an unordered set rather than a ranked \LIMIT~$K$ prefix. OrderByLLM~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2509-00303} studies access-path selection across pointwise, pairwise, and two-way external merge sort, capping intermediate runs at $K$ for \LIMIT~$K$. In our framework, two-way half-window merge corresponds to the single point $\mathrm{SW}(2,\lfloor m/2\rfloor)$; we generalize external sorting~\cite{DBLP:journals/csur/Graefe06} to parameterized multi-way SW and LT($h$) mergers, propagate \LIMIT~$K$ demand lazily from the root, and compile intra-operator $(f,k,h)$ plans via a finite-execution input-token cost model.

\stitle{Preference-Graph Reuse and Noisy Comparators.}
Transitive Batched Sort (TBS)~\cite{akinola2026tbs} maintains a sparse direct-edge preference DAG with a dynamic topological index to skip scheduled batches whose pairwise relations are all transitively implied. While effective at reducing calls relative to fixed two-way merge in Full sorting, maintaining a global preference graph differs fundamentally from cost-planned multi-way merging in four respects. First, TBS's fixed-$m$ grid and myopic residual packing ($\arg\max_{c\notin B}|U[c]\cap B|$) share the $\Omega(\frac{n}{m}\log_m n)$ call bound with multi-way merge rather than solving optimal subset cover: batches containing even one unresolved pair must still resubmit all known tuples (inflating item cost $L$), while isolated residual pairs produce underfilled calls that pay full header overhead $H$. Second, TBS targets blocking Full ranking—extracting order via global topological sort at termination, where pairwise coverage early stopping ($\theta$) does not certify an ordered top-$K$ prefix. Third, TBS relies on labeled ground-truth sweeps to calibrate $m$, whereas our planner profiles $(H,L)$ online without labels or LLM generation and adapts naturally to KV prefix caching. Finally, under inconsistent judgments, TBS rejects cycle-inducing edges (preserving acyclicity at the risk of first-come transitive lock-in), whereas LT enforces local run-chain monotonicity and SW permits cross-round survivor re-ranking—representing distinct structural trade-offs evaluated under unified protocols.
